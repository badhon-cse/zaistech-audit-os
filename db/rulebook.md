# Zaistech Site Audit & Outreach Rulebook

This is the standing playbook for auditing a prospect's online store and writing outreach for it. Follow it every time so the work is consistent and nobody has to explain it again. Update it when services, proof or process change (see Changelog at the bottom).

## 0. Ground rules

- **Only audit when the user asks.** The user picks leads one at a time from the lead sheet. Never open, browse, or audit sites on your own initiative, and never batch through the sheet.
- **One site per request** unless the user names several.
- **Test like a shopper, never pay.** The audit includes real tests: subscribing to the store's email signup (welcome flow), adding a product to cart and entering the audit email at checkout (abandoned-cart flow), and viewing cart/checkout upsells. Use ONLY the audit email (§2.1). At checkout enter the email (and a first name if required), then stop. Never enter card/payment details, never place an order, never create an account with a password, never enter a real home address or phone number. If checkout won't save the email without phone/address, skip the test and note "couldn't test".
- **Confirm before submitting.** At the start of each audit tell the user in one line: "I'll subscribe <audit email> on <site> and enter it at checkout (no payment) — OK?" and wait for a yes. The yes covers that one site only.
- **Only claim what you saw.** Every problem in the audit must be something you observed on the page, with where you saw it. If a check is inconclusive (e.g. you can't tell whether an abandoned-cart flow exists without signing up), say "couldn't verify" and don't put it in the outreach.
- **Be useful, not insulting.** Frame findings as missed revenue or quick wins, never as "your site is bad".
- Outreach messages are always in **English**. Talk to the user in Banglish/Bangla, matching how they write.

## 3. How to audit (step by step)

Use the built-in browser (or Claude in Chrome if the user prefers). Each new site needs the user's approval: call request_access for that one site, wait, then continue. Never fire several access requests in parallel.

Two phases: **Phase 1** is the live visit (today). **Phase 2** is the inbox check (welcome: 15–30 min later; abandoned cart: ~1h, ~4h and ~24h later). Draft outreach after Phase 1, finalize after Phase 2.

### Phase 1 — live visit (in this order)
1. **Homepage scan** — open the domain, wait ~4s, run §3.1 and §3.3. Note platform and tools. Write down 4–6 genuine strengths as you go (real photo shoots, detailed listings, published policies, years in business, Trustpilot/Google/Instagram numbers, payment options) — useful for the demo and for opening lines.
2. **Welcome-flow test** — find the signup (popup, bar, footer). Note the offer, after how many seconds the popup appears, whether it is full-screen and on every page, and how it looks on mobile. After the user's OK, subscribe with the audit email; record the time and the promised incentive.
3. **Product page** — open 1–2 real best-sellers (skip gift cards). Run §3.2 and read the page: headline, benefits, images/video, reviews near Add to Cart, trust (guarantee, shipping, returns — next to the price or on separate pages?), BNPL, FAQ/size guide, subscription, conflicting info, PDP upsells.
4. **Cart** — add one item: free-shipping bar, cart upsell/cross-sell, trust, payment icons, bugs.
5. **Checkout + abandoned-cart test** — note express pay options (Shop Pay, PayPal, Apple/Google Pay), guest vs forced account, surprise fees, checkout upsells, trust, speed. Enter the audit email (+ first name if required), tab out so it saves, then **stop and close the tab**. Never fill payment or place an order. Record the time. Post-purchase upsells → "couldn't verify".
6. **Mobile check** — mobile preset, reload homepage + PDP, screenshot (useful as "before" shots in a demo). Check popup, sticky Add to Cart, readability, how long until the main product photo shows. Reset to desktop.
7. **Other key pages** — a collection page (filters, cards, intro text); the About page (heavy autoplay video?); any money page specific to the business (consign, wholesale, quiz) — do customers have to do work themselves?
8. **PageSpeed Insights** — open https://pagespeed.web.dev/analysis?url=<URL>&form_factor=mobile for the homepage and one PDP; wait up to ~60s. Record mobile Performance score, LCP, INP, CLS, TBT, total requests and page weight, and the top opportunities (fonts, unused JS, oversized images/video, render-blocking apps). Note the test conditions (mobile, slow 4G emulation, date).
9. **Ads check** — Meta Ad Library: https://www.facebook.com/ads/library/?active_status=active&ad_type=all&country=ALL&q=<brand>&search_type=keyword_unordered (pick the right page). Record: active ads yes/no, count, how long the oldest has run, formats, main offer, landing page used. Optional: Google Ads Transparency Center, TikTok Creative Center.
10. **Outside signals** — web search "<brand> reviews", Trustpilot, Google rating, Instagram followers, years in business.

### Phase 2 — inbox check (the user checks Gmail and reports)
- **Welcome:** did an email arrive within ~30 min? Was the promised code delivered? How many emails over 3–7 days (a real flow sends 3–5)? Design, mobile rendering, subjects, education, CTA.
- **Abandoned cart:** reminder at ~1h / ~4h / ~24h? How many? Product shown, link back to cart, reviews/urgency/incentive? Nothing within 24h → top finding. A single plain platform default → "needs work", not "missing".
- Give the user a pending-checks list (site, what was triggered, when, when to check). Offer a scheduled reminder if available.

## 4. What to look for (checklist → service)

Go through every section. Record each finding as: **What** (observed fact) · **Where** (page/URL) · **Why it costs money** · **Fix** · **Our service**.

### A. Email & SMS capture → Email marketing
- Is there an email signup (popup, footer, inline)? Is there an incentive (e.g. 10% off)? Does the popup appear on mobile without blocking the page?
- Which email tool is loaded (Klaviyo, Omnisend, Mailchimp, Privy, none)? No tool or only Mailchimp → strong email pitch. Klaviyo present → pitch optimization of flows/campaigns, not setup.
- SMS tool (Attentive, Postscript)? If email exists but no SMS, note it but don't lead with it.
- Restricted category (peptides, CBD/cannabis, kratom, vapes, some supplements)? → mention Inboly.
- Welcome and abandoned-cart flows are verified only by the Phase 1 tests + Phase 2 check. Before the wait window ends, say "I signed up on <date> — will let you know what lands" or leave it out.
- **Welcome missing/weak** → lead with a welcome series that sells (story + code, best-sellers, education, social proof, last chance on the code). Cite proof and the 7-day / 30%-in-45-days guarantees.
- **Abandoned cart missing/plain** → pitch a 2–3 email recovery flow (reminder with product image → reviews/objection handling → incentive/urgency) + browse abandonment.
- Popup that is full-screen, on every page, after a few seconds → finding; fix = a lighter, once-per-visitor prompt.

### B. Customer support & chat → ZAIS Chat
- Is there a chat widget? Which one (Gorgias, Tidio, Zendesk, Shopify Inbox, none)?
- No chat, or a basic chat with no AI → pitch ZAIS Chat.
- Lots of questions answered in FAQ pages, size charts, shipping tables → pitch ZAIS answering them instantly 24/7, incl. WhatsApp/Instagram.
- Gorgias/Zendesk already present → lighter pitch (AI layer, multichannel), or skip.

### C. Reviews & social proof → Email (review requests) + product page fixes
- Are star ratings visible on product cards and near Add to Cart? How many reviews on a top product?
- No reviews at all, or very few vs. brand size → pitch post-purchase review-request flow.
- Sitewide claim (e.g. "5,000+ reviews") but low count on product pages → surface reviews better.
- Multiple review apps loaded → possible leftovers slowing the site.

### D. Product page (PDP) → Website fixes/development + checkout friction
- Clear benefit-led headline and description (not just specs/ingredient list)?
- Trust near Add to Cart: guarantee, shipping time, returns, payment icons, BNPL.
- Conflicting info (e.g. "no returns" and "easy returns" on the same page) — high-value finding, always report.
- Size guide (apparel), FAQ, how-to-use, comparison, UGC/photos.
- Subscription offer for consumables (supplements, coffee, skincare) — missing → note.
- Sticky Add to Cart on mobile.
- Bundles/upsells/cross-sells.

### E. SEO (Google & AI) → SEO service
- Homepage: exactly one meaningful H1 (missing or empty H1 is common).
- Title tags: unique, descriptive, < ~60 characters.
- Meta description: present, written for clicks (not raw ingredients, not truncated mid-sentence, not a subscription-only blurb).
- Duplicate H1s on product pages.
- Images missing alt text (report as "X of Y images").
- Product schema present? With aggregateRating? Missing → rich-snippet stars lost.
- Collection pages with no intro text.
- AI visibility: optionally ask a web search "best <category> brands" and see if they appear; mention "when people ask ChatGPT where to buy <category>".

### F. Speed & technical → Website fixes
- **PageSpeed Insights is the primary source** (Phase 1 step 8). Mobile score 90+ good, 50–89 needs work, < 50 poor. LCP ≤ 2.5s good, > 4s poor. INP ≤ 200ms good, > 500ms poor. CLS ≤ 0.1 good, > 0.25 poor.
- Translate into customer terms: "product photos take ~X s to appear on a phone", "the page loads N files (X MB), including K font families", "the About page loads a ~X MB video".
- Script loadMs is a backup only: > 4,000 ms slow, > 7,000 ms very slow; phrase as "took about X seconds to load when I visited".
- Page weight (transferKB) > 5 MB heavy; scripts count > 120 heavy (app bloat).
- Broken links, 404s, console errors (read_console_messages, onlyErrors), layout bugs on mobile.
- Announcement bar repeated/marquee bloat, oversized hero images.

### G. Checkout & conversion friction → Checkout & friction service
- Free-shipping threshold visible? Shipping cost/time clear before cart?
- Payment options: Shop Pay, PayPal, Apple Pay, BNPL.
- Cart: free-shipping progress bar, upsell, trust message.
- Checkout: guest checkout allowed? Forced account creation? Surprise fees? Trust badges? Slow or broken steps? Distracting links out of checkout?

### G2. Upsells & AOV → Website development + email
- PDP: bundles, quantity breaks, "frequently bought together", subscription upsell.
- Cart: cross-sell/upsell block, free-shipping bar that nudges order value.
- Checkout: add-on offers.
- Post-purchase: one-click upsell (couldn't verify without paying) and post-purchase cross-sell emails.
- No upsell anywhere → pitch bundles + cart upsell + post-purchase flow.

### H. Ads & tracking → Ads service
- From the Ad Library check (Phase 1 step 9) plus pixels detected (Meta, TikTok, GTM).
- Running ads + slow/weak landing page or no welcome/abandoned flow → strongest angle: "you're paying for traffic that leaks".
- Few creatives, only statics, or the same ads for months → pitch fresh creative (UGC-style video, offer-led statics).
- Pixels installed but no active ads → ads paused; pitch relaunch with creative + landing fixes.
- No pixels and no ads → lead with email/SEO, mention ads lightly.

### I2. Business-specific friction → Development / AI automation
- Any page where customers must do work themselves (calculate payouts, request quotes by email, dig sizes out of a PDF) → calculator, quiz, instant quote, or ZAIS/GoHighLevel automation.
- Homepage wording that doesn't match the business → rewrite.

### I. Seasonal / timing hooks
- Current sale (Black Friday, holiday, launch) visible? Use it as the opening hook ("saw your BF early access is live…").

## 5. Prioritize

Pick the **top 3 findings** for outreach using this order:
1. Concrete, verifiable, and specific to their site (a number, a quote, a page).
2. Direct revenue impact (email capture, reviews, conflicting policy, slow PDP).
3. Maps cleanly to one of our services/products.
4. Easy for the reader to verify in 10 seconds.

Match the angle to the contact's **title**:
- Founder / CEO / Co-founder → revenue, time saved, proof numbers.
- CMO / Marketing / Retention / Ecommerce manager → specific fixes, email/SEO detail, speed.
- Ops / IT / Ecommerce & IT director → speed, app bloat, support load (ZAIS Chat).

## 6. The demo (personalized for every site)

The demo is the main attention-getter: a piece made for one owner that shows, using their own store, what we'd fix and what it would look like. **There is no fixed template.** Every demo is designed fresh for that site, around what the audit actually found and what that brand cares about. Suggest one after every audit; build it when the user says go.

### 6.1 Deciding what to build
1. Start from the audit's top 1–3 findings and the biggest money leak. The demo should make that leak obvious and show the fix.
2. Pick the form that proves it best for this brand. Examples, not a list to follow:
   - Welcome flow missing → their welcome email series designed in their brand, with their products and offer.
   - Abandoned cart missing → the recovery emails they'd send, using the product we left in the cart.
   - Weak product page → their best-seller page rebuilt, side by side with the current one.
   - Slow mobile site → a fast concept page plus a before/after speed comparison measured on both.
   - No chat / lots of FAQs → ZAIS Chat answering real questions from their own policies.
   - No upsells → their PDP/cart with bundles and cross-sells added.
   - Ads running to a weak page → a landing page built for their current ad offer.
   - A combination, or something else that fits the brand better.
3. Match the look and voice to the brand (colors, type, photography, tone), not to a house style.
4. Agree the plan with the user in a few lines (what it shows, which findings, rough look) before building.

### 6.2 What a good demo usually has (use what fits)
- A short, sincere note on what they do well, so it doesn't read as an attack.
- The problems, each with a number or a screenshot and a one-line fix.
- The "after": a mockup or working concept built from their real content.
- Measured before/after numbers where speed or performance is the point (method stated).
- What working with Zaistech would look like and one simple next step.
Length, order and sections are up to each demo; keep it short enough for a busy owner to get in under two minutes.

### 6.3 Demo rules (always)
- Clearly labeled as a concept by Zaistech and not the brand's live site (e.g. a banner "A concept for <Brand> by Zaistech · Not the live site"), so it can never be mistaken for their real site or emails. Web pages are noindex,nofollow and unlisted.
- Use only the brand's real content (photos, products, prices, policies, numbers). Never invent reviews, prices, claims or stats. Generated visuals are labeled "for illustration".
- Forms are non-functional placeholders; Sale/Account/checkout links go to their live site. Never collect visitor data.
- Every number is measured, with the method stated.
- Build as files (HTML, images) and hand them to the user to host or send. Because demos carry the brand's identity, don't publish them anywhere yourself; the user decides where they go.
- Tone: plain, direct, confident, consultative; "we" and "you"; concrete figures; warm, not salesy.

## 7. Output format

Return, in this order:

**1. Audit summary (for the user, in Banglish/Bangla + English terms)**
- Lead: name, title, company, domain, platform, tools detected.
- Findings table: # · Area · What we found (with page) · Impact · Fix · Zaistech service. Include everything observed, ranked.
- "Couldn't verify" list.
- Recommended pitch: main service + secondary (e.g. Email flows + ZAIS Chat) and which proof to cite.

**1b. Pending checks** — what was triggered (signup, checkout email), when, and when the user should check Gmail.

**1c. Demo idea** — a personalized demo suggestion for this site (§6.1): what it shows, which findings, the form, rough look. Build on the user's go.

**2. Cold email (English)**
- Subject: ≤ 6 words, lowercase-friendly, specific to them (e.g. "cowboy colostrum's product pages").
- Body: 70–120 words. Line 1 = a specific observation about their site (not a compliment template). 1–2 findings with the money angle. One proof line from §1 matched to their niche. One soft CTA: offer the free audit video ("want me to send a 3-min video walkthrough?"). No attachments, no links in the first email except optionally our site, no "I hope this finds you well".
- Follow-up 1 (day 3): 40–60 words, a different finding or the guarantee.
- Follow-up 2 (day 7): 25–40 words, breakup-style, keep the door open.
- Signature placeholder: [Your name], Zaistech, [link].

**3. LinkedIn**
- Connection note ≤ 300 characters: one specific observation + why connecting.
- Message after accept: 50–80 words, one finding, one proof, soft CTA.

**4. Instagram / Facebook DM**
- 30–60 words, casual, compliment one real thing about the brand, one quick finding, ask if they'd like the video. No hard sell.

### Writing rules
- Use their first name and company name; mention a real product of theirs.
- Never invent stats about their store; use "when I checked" phrasing for our observations.
- Our proof numbers must match §1 exactly; don't inflate.
- Don't mention competitors negatively; don't mention tools they use negatively ("your Klaviyo setup is bad").
- One CTA per message.
- Plain text, no emojis in email; at most one emoji in IG/FB DM.
- Don't mention BuyMyWeedUSA unless the prospect is cannabis-adjacent.
- When a demo exists, the soft CTA becomes an offer to send it (e.g. "I mocked up what your welcome emails could look like — want me to send it over?"). Send the demo on reply rather than in the first cold email, unless the user prefers otherwise.
